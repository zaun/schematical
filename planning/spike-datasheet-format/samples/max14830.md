# MAX14830 quad serial UART with 128-word FIFOs

## Description

Four UARTs behind one SPI (up to 26 MHz, mode 0) or I²C (up to 1 MHz,
Fast-mode Plus) host interface. Each UART has 128-word transmit and
receive FIFOs, a 16-bit integer plus 4-bit fractional baud-rate
generator, 2× and 4× rate modes (up to 6 Mbaud at 16× sampling, 12/24
Mbaud in 2×/4× mode), four GPIOs with 20 mA drive, and a SIR/MIR IrDA
encoder/decoder. The UART is picked by two address bits in the SPI
command byte or by the I²C slave address.

Automatic functions take time-critical work off the host: RTS/CTS and
XON/XOFF flow control, RS-485 transceiver direction control with
programmable setup and hold times, echo suppression, 9-bit multidrop
address filtering, special-character detection, and transmitter start
synchronized across UARTs (and across chips, over SPI) by global
trigger commands. A crystal oscillator or external clock, a predivider
and a PLL feed all four baud-rate generators.

Supplies: V~A~ 2.35 V to 3.6 V (oscillator, PLL, LDO), an internal or
external 1.8 V core supply (V~18~), and separate logic-level supplies
for the host side (V~L~) and the transceiver side (V~EXT~), each 1.71 V
to 3.6 V. Register compatible with the MAX3107, MAX3108 and MAX3109.
–40 °C to 85 °C.

## Functional description

Block diagram: the SPI/I²C interface (with level translation from V~L~)
reaches the registers and control logic of four identical UARTs; each
UART's transmitter, receiver, flow control and four GPIOs connect
through level translators (V~EXT~) to the pins. A crystal oscillator,
predivider and PLL feed the four fractional baud-rate generators; an
LDO makes the 1.8 V core supply.

Everything below describes one UART unless said otherwise. Registers
are 8 bits wide and organized by function (see the
[register map](#registers)).

### FIFOs

The transmitter and the receiver each have a 128-word FIFO. Trigger
levels (FIFOTrgLvl) raise interrupts at programmed fill levels, so the
host can move data in blocks with SPI or I²C bursts; TxFIFOLvl and
RxFIFOLvl can be read at any time, and FIFORst clears both FIFOs.

### Transmitter

The host writes characters into THR; they go through the transmit FIFO
and are sent on TX_ with START, parity and STOP bits added. ISR[5]
(TFifoEmptyInt) is set when the transmitter starts sending the last
word in the FIFO, so the transmitter is completely empty one character
time (START, data, parity and STOP bits) later. Everything here
assumes TX_ is not inverted (TxInv = 0).

Writing THR while the transmitter is sending can corrupt the transmit
FIFO, so that a character is not transmitted. Avoid it by disabling the
transmitter (MODE1[1]) while writing THR.

### Receiver

The receiver detects a START bit as a falling edge on RX_ (idle high)
and samples each bit 16 times per bit period; the logic level is the
majority of the three samples nearest the middle of the bit. Each
received word is stored in the receive FIFO together with its error
flags (parity, framing, noise). The host reads the oldest word from
RHR; LSR then holds that word's status.

**Line noise.** In 16× and 2× rate modes (not 4×), the three samples of
each bit must agree; if they don't, RxNoise is set for that word. Parity errors also indicate noise, but less sensitively.

### Clocking

The clock comes from a crystal (CLKSource[1] CrystalEn = 1,
BRGConfig[6] CLKDisabl = 0) or from an external clock on XIN
(CrystalEn = 0, XOUT open). The crystal oscillator has 16 pF (typical)
load capacitance built into each of XIN and XOUT; a crystal or ceramic
resonator connects between them. With a crystal, STSInt[5]
(ClockReady) reports when the clocks have settled.

The predivider (PLLConfig[5:0], 1 to 63) and PLL (×6, ×48, ×96 or ×144,
PLLConfig[7:6]) between the clock input and the baud-rate generators
allow a wide range of input frequencies; CLKSource[3:2] enable or
bypass them. The resulting reference f~REF~ drives all four baud-rate
generators. Each UART's baud rate is programmed separately; for a fast
baud-rate change, set CLKDisabl, change the divisor, then clear
CLKDisabl.

To check the clock programming, route the baud-rate clock to RTS_
(CLKtoRTS). With a fractional divisor this clock is irregular (jitter).

### Fractional baud-rate generators

Each generator has a 16-bit integer divisor DIV (DIVMSB, DIVLSB;
minimum 1) and a 4-bit fractional divisor FRACT (BRGConfig[3:0],
resolution 1/16):

```equation
D = f~REF~ / (16 × BaudRate)
DIV = TRUNC(D)
FRACT = ROUND(16 × (D – DIV))
BR~ACTUAL~ = f~REF~ / (16 × (DIV + FRACT/16))
```

In 2× and 4× rate modes, replace 16 by 8 or 4 in the first equation.

Example: 190 kbaud from f~REF~ = 28.23 MHz, 16× mode: D = 28 230 000 /
(16 × 190 000) = 9.286, so DIV = 9 (DIVMSB = 0x00, DIVLSB = 0x09) and
FRACT = ROUND(4.579) = 0x05. The actual rate is 28 230 000 / (16 ×
9.3125) = 189 463 baud, within 0.28 % of the target.

### 2× and 4× rate modes

BRGConfig[5:4] select 2× or 4× rate mode, in which f~REF~ needs to be
only 8× or 4× the baud rate: the line rate is twice or four times the
rate programmed into the generator. In 4× mode each bit is sampled once
at mid-bit instead of three times, which reduces noise tolerance. IrDA
encoding and decoding don't work in 2× and 4× modes.

Each UART also has a low-frequency timer for a GPIO output (TIMER1,
TIMER2) and can put its reference clock on a GPIO (TxSynch[7]).

### Multidrop mode

In 9-bit mode (MultiDrop) a word is 8 data bits plus a 9th bit that
marks an address word, and MultiDropInt flags each received address.
The host filters the data, or uses auto data filtering:

**Auto data filtering** (MODE2[6] = 1 and MODE2[4] SpecialChr = 1):
the UART puts only data addressed to it into the receive FIFO and
discards the rest; the address word itself is not stored. The address
is XOFF2, or, with FlowCtrl[2] (GPIAddr) also set, XOFF2[7:4] as the
upper nibble and the UART's four GPIO inputs as the lower nibble. The
GPIOs are read automatically and the address follows their changes.

### Auto transceiver direction control

For half-duplex RS-485, or full-duplex multidrop buses where only one
transmitter may drive, MODE1[4] (TrnscvCtrl) makes RTS_ drive the
transceiver's driver enable: RTS_ goes high as soon as the transmit
FIFO holds data, before transmission starts, and low after the
transmitter is empty, with setup and hold times from HDplxDelay
([timing](#timing-diagrams)). Typical connection: TX_ to the
transceiver's DI, RX_ to RO, RTS_ to DE and !{RE} (MAX14840E).

### Transmitter triggering and synchronization

A UART's transmitter can wait for one of 16 trigger commands (0xE0 to
0xEF written to GloblComnd, received by all four UARTs at once whatever
the U1, U0 bits) and start sending when it arrives, if it is disabled
and its transmit FIFO holds data. TxSynch selects the command (TrigSel),
enables it (SynchEn) and optionally adds a delay (TrigDelay, with
SynchDelay1/2 in bit times). The transmitter must be disabled before
the trigger: MODE1[1] = 1 or auto transmitter disable (TxSynch[6]).

UARTs given the same trigger command start together. Within one chip
this works over SPI and I²C. Across chips it works over SPI only: pull
!{CS} of all the MAX14830s low while the master writes the trigger
command. (I²C can't address several devices at once.)

**Trigger accuracy.** The delay from receiving the trigger (the 16th
SCLK rising edge) to the START bit has a fixed and a random part, both
set by the UART's clock and baud rate. Without the fractional divider,
in any rate mode, with BR the fractional divider output clock period:

```equation
5 × BR / 16 ≤ t~TRIG~ ≤ 6 × BR / 16
```

With the fractional divider in use, the random part exceeds one UART
clock period. For several UARTs, the skew between their TX_ outputs,
with BR~S~ and BR~F~ the divider output clock periods of the slower and
faster UART, is:

```equation
t~TRIGSKEW~(max) ≤ (6 × BR~S~ – 5 × BR~F~) / 16
```

### Echo suppression

EchoSuprs blocks local echoes in half-duplex links (RS-485, IrDA) whose
transceiver receiver stays on while transmitting; the hold time covers
long round-trip delays. It works together with auto transceiver
direction control.

### Auto hardware flow control

AutoRTS (FlowCtrl[0]) and AutoCTS (FlowCtrl[1]) work independently and
without the host:

- **AutoRTS** keeps the receive FIFO from overflowing: RTS_ is
  deasserted when the receive FIFO fills to the HALT level
  (FlowLvl[3:0]) and asserted again when it drains to the RESUME level
  (FlowLvl[7:4]); different levels give hysteresis. No interrupts are
  raised at these levels.
- **AutoCTS** starts the transmitter while CTS_ is low and stops it,
  after the current word, while CTS_ is high. The CTS interrupt (ISR[7])
  works as usual; clear IRQEn[7] to mask it. Disable the transmitter
  (MODE1[1] = 1) before enabling AutoCTS.

FIFO trigger levels (FIFOTrgLvl) are independent of HALT and RESUME;
an interrupt (ISR[3], ISR[4]) is raised when a FIFO level reaches or
passes its trigger level, useful for block transfers.

### Auto software (XON/XOFF) flow control

FlowCtrl[7:3] enable automatic XON/XOFF handling with the characters in
XON1, XON2, XOFF1 and XOFF2; double-width (16-bit) XON/XOFF reduces the
chance of a corrupted data byte matching. Receiving XON or XOFF raises
ISR[1], with details in SpclCharInt (mask with IRQEn[1]). The two parts
work independently:

- **Transmitter flow control** (FlowCtrl[5:4]): received words are
  compared with XOFF and XON; XOFF halts the transmitter, XON restarts
  it. The receiver keeps receiving. XON and XOFF are not put into the
  receive FIFO, and raise no interrupt. Disable the transmitter
  (MODE1[1] = 1) before enabling this.
- **Receiver overflow control** (FlowCtrl[7:6]): XOFF is sent when the
  receive FIFO reaches the HALT level, XON when the host has read it
  down to the RESUME level (FlowLvl). With two-character flow control,
  XON1/XOFF1 is sent before XON2/XOFF2.

### Power-up, reset and shutdown

During power-up or after a reset, !{IRQ} is held low until the part is
ready to be programmed, then goes high; the host then sets MODE1[7]
(IRQSel) to make !{IRQ} a normal interrupt output, which is active
while an interrupt is pending and enabled in IRQEn. In polled mode,
read DIVLSB until it returns a valid value.

Pulling !{RST} low enters shutdown, the lowest-power mode: all
circuitry is off, including the SPI/I²C interface, registers, FIFOs and
clocks; the LDO stays on. When !{RST} goes high the part initializes and
sets !{IRQ} high when done. It must be reprogrammed after shutdown.

### Interrupts

Each UART has four interrupt source registers: ISR, LSR, STSInt and
SpclCharInt. ISR holds the top-level (frequent) interrupts directly;
its three LSBs point to the low-level registers LSR (ISR[0]),
SpclCharInt (ISR[1]) and STSInt (ISR[2]). GlobalIRQ shows which UART is
interrupting. Every interrupt bit has an enable bit in IRQEn, LSRIntEn,
SpclChrIntEn or STSIntEn.

Reading ISR clears its bits and !{IRQ}. SpclCharInt and STSInt are
also cleared on read; LSR bits are cleared only when their cause goes
away. Reading GlobalIRQ clears nothing.

### SPI interface

The SPI master uses mode 0 (CPOL = 0, CPHA = 0). Each transfer starts
with a command byte: R/W, the UART address U1:U0 (00 = UART0 … 11 =
UART3) and the register address A4:A0.

```table id=spi-cmd caption="SPI command byte"
| Bit 7 | Bit 6 | Bit 5 | Bit 4 | Bit 3 | Bit 2 | Bit 1 | Bit 0 |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| W/!{R} | U1 | U0 | A4 | A3 | A2 | A1 | A0 |
```

MISO is high impedance for a whole write cycle, and for the first four
clocks of a read cycle, because all four UARTs could drive it until the
UART address is decoded. In a read, the next four MISO bits carry
!{IRQ3}…!{IRQ0} (GlobalIRQ[3:0]) and then the register data.

**Burst access.** Keep !{CS} low and keep clocking after the first data
byte. With register address 0x00, every byte goes to THR (write) or
comes from RHR (read), filling or emptying the FIFO; with any other
address the address increments after each byte. Writing 128 bytes to a
transmit FIFO takes (1 + 128) × 8 clocks.

**Fast read cycle.** A read cut off after the command byte returns
!{IRQ3}…!{IRQ0} in 8 clocks, so the host finds the interrupting UART
quickly. U1 and U0 are ignored.

**Extended addressing.** The command byte has five address bits, so
registers 0x20–0x25 need extended mode in SPI: write 0xCE to GloblComnd
to make the internal address 0010 A3A2A1A0 (A4 ignored, U1:U0 still
select the UART), and 0xCD to return to 000A4 A3A2A1A0 (default). I²C
reaches all registers directly.

### I²C interface

The I²C slave supports clocks up to 1 MHz; SCL and SDA need pull-ups.
SDA is stable while SCL is high, except for START (S, SDA falls while
SCL is high), repeated START (Sr) and STOP (P, SDA rises while SCL is
high). Both lines are high when the bus is idle. Every byte is followed
by an acknowledge bit: the receiver pulls SDA low before the rising
edge of the 9th clock and holds it through the high period (ACK), or
leaves it high (NACK).

The 7-bit slave address, set by connecting MOSI/A1 and !{CS}/A0 to
DGND, V~L~, SCL or SDA, also selects the UART, so up to 16 MAX14830s
share a bus. The R/!{W} bit is 1 for a read.

### RHR, THR (0x00)

Reading RHR returns, and removes, the oldest character in the receive
FIFO; RData0 is the first data bit received. A character written to THR
goes into the transmit FIFO; TData0 is sent first, right after the
START bit.

### IRQEn (0x01)

Bit n of IRQEn set to 1 lets ISR bit n drive !{IRQ} (names in the
[map](#registers)); it does not change ISR itself.

### ISR (0x02)

Bit n (5:0) set to 1 routes LSR bit n to ISR[0] (LSRErrInt).

### LSR (0x04)

Bits 5:1 describe the word most recently read from RHR; they stay set
until a word without that error is read, and are not cleared by
reading LSR.

### XON1, XON2, XOFF1, XOFF2 (0x14–0x17)

Each holds one character. XON1/XOFF1 are the single-character XON/XOFF,
or the low byte of a two-character XON/XOFF; XON2/XOFF2 the single
character or the high byte. With special character detection on and
auto flow control off, all four are special characters; with both on,
XON1/XOFF1 are the flow-control characters and XON2/XOFF2 special
characters. XOFF2 is also the multidrop address for auto data
filtering.


### SynchDelay1 (0x21), SynchDelay2 (0x22)

SDelay[15:0] (SynchDelay2 high byte) is the delay from trigger command
to start of transmission when TrigDelay = 1, in bit times (1/baud
rate), up to 65 535: at 230.4 kbps (4.34 µs) that is 284 ms.

### TIMER1 (0x23), TIMER2 (0x24)

Timer[14:0] (TIMER2[6:0] high bits, TIMER1 low byte) divides the
UART clock; TIMER1 = TIMER2 = 0x00 turns the timer off. TmrToGPIO
(TIMER2[7]) = 1 puts the timer clock (50 % duty cycle, for example for
LEDs) on GPIO1 (UART0), GPIO5, GPIO9 or GPIO13 (UART3).

```equation
f~TIMER_CLK~ = UARTClk / (1024 × Timer)
```

UARTClk is the fractional baud-rate generator output: 16×, 8× or 4×
the baud rate in 16×, 2× or 4× mode.

### RevID (0x25)

Silicon revision (0xB4 in this revision); revisions start at 0xB1.

## Application information

### Startup and initialization

After power-up or a hardware or software reset: wait until !{IRQ} is
high (interrupt-driven) or until DIVLSB, or another known register,
reads back its expected value (polled); then configure clocking, modes,
FIFO control, flow control and GPIOs, enable interrupts, and start
communicating.

### Low-power operation

- Don't use the PLL (disable and bypass it); this saves the most.
- Stop the clock of unused UARTs with CLKDisabl.
- Supply V~18~ from an external 1.8 V supply and turn off the internal
  regulator (LDOEN to DGND).
- Keep internal clock rates as low as possible.
- Use a low V~A~.

### Interrupts and polling

Either poll ISR at frequent intervals (!{IRQ} unused), or let the
open-drain !{IRQ}, with a pull-up to V~L~, interrupt the host on the
enabled events.

### Logic-level translation

V~L~ sets the host-side logic levels and V~EXT~ the transceiver-side
levels, so the host, the MAX14830 and the transceivers can run from
different supplies: for example a 1.8 V microcontroller on V~L~, 3.3 V
on V~A~, and a 2.5 V MAX3078 transceiver on V~EXT~ (TX_ to DI, RX_ to
RO, RTS_ to DE).

### Interchip synchronization

Several MAX14830s can share one clock: one chip runs the crystal
oscillator and puts its UART clock on a GPIO (TxSynch[7]); the others
take it on XIN with the crystal oscillator and PLL off. Together with
trigger commands sent to all chips at once over SPI, their transmitters
start in step.

### Typical circuits

- **IO-Link quad master:** one controller on SPI (with a separate !{CS}
  and !{RST}); TX_, RX_ and RTS_ of each UART drive a MAX14824 IO-Link
  transceiver's TXC, RX and TXEN; GPIO1, GPIO5, GPIO9 and GPIO13 go to
  the transceivers' address inputs; crystal on XIN/XOUT.
- **Quad RS-485 over SPI:** 3.3 V on V~A~, V~EXT~ and V~L~ (0.1 µF
  bypass each, 1 µF on V~18~), LDOEN high, SPI/!{I2C} high. Each UART
  drives a MAX14840 transceiver: TX_ to DI, RX_ to RO, RTS_ to DE and
  !{RE}; !{IRQ} to the microcontroller.
