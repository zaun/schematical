# IS31FL3707

## What it is

I2C 8 x 8 / 7 x 9 LED matrix driver, 8 current sinks CS1-CS8 and 9 high-side switches SW1-SW9 (`is31fl3707` p.1). Each dot has an 8-bit
PWM byte (256 steps), each CSy an 8-bit current-scaling byte, plus a 7-bit global current and an ISET resistor. Open and short detection per
dot, PWM clock 0.25 kHz to 220 kHz, spread spectrum, 180 degree phase shift, de-ghost pull-down / pull-up voltages (p.1, p.13-15). Supply
2.7 V to 5.5 V (p.5 VCC), -40 to +125 C (p.1). Max sink current of a CSy 35 mA at RISET 10 kOhm, GCC 40h, SL FFh (p.5). Logic: VIL max 0.6 V,
VIH min 1.6 V, VIH of the I2C bus must stay below VCC (p.1 note 1, p.6).

## I2C (p.8-9, Figures 6-8)

- 7-bit slave address A7:A1, then R/W (A0): A7:A3 = 01101, A2:A1 set by AD (Table 1, p.8):

| AD tied to | A2:A1 | 7-bit address | write byte / read byte |
|---|---|---|---|
| GND | 00 | 0x68 | 0xD0 / 0xD1 |
| SCL | 01 | 0x69 | 0xD2 / 0xD3 |
| SDA | 10 | 0x6A | 0xD4 / 0xD5 |
| VCC | 11 | 0x6B | 0xD6 / 0xD7 |

  Engine `bus.table` (index = tie digit, 0 GND, 1 VDD, 2 SCL, 3 SDA): `[104, 107, 105, 106]`. A floating AD is not in the table.
- Write (Figure 6): START, address (A0 = 0), register address byte, data byte, STOP; every byte acknowledged by the chip.
- Auto-increment (Figure 7, p.8-9): after each data byte "the internal address pointer will increment by one" and the next byte goes to the new
  address, as long as data continues. The pointer steps by one address, not to the next documented register.
- Read (Figure 8): START, address with R/W = 0, register address, restart, address with R/W = 1, data byte, NAK, STOP. "Most of the registers
  can be read" (p.8). The sheet does not say whether a read continues to the next address (the model does); it does not say what reserved
  addresses read (the model: 0, writes dropped).
- SDA is open drain, pull-up 4.7 kOhm at 400 kHz, 2 kOhm at 1 MHz (p.8); fSCL up to 1 MHz (p.6).

## Scan and shutdown (p.16-18)

- The switches turn on one after another; the row is high while its LEDs may light, the sinks pull the lit columns low (SWx is "source and it is high when LED on", p.16). Each
  row slot is tSCAN + tNOL1 + tNOL2 = 29 + 0.6 + 0.33 = 29.93 us at B2h = 01h (34 kHz): "Scanning cycle T = 59.86 us (n = 2), 239.44 us (n = 8), 269.37 us (n = 9)" (p.16 Figure 11
  and text; tSCAN 29 us, p.5; tNOL1 0.6 us, tNOL2 0.33 us, p.6). At 220 kHz tSCAN is 4.5-5.25 us (p.5). The model uses 29.93 us.
- PWM: "The brightness of each LED can be modulated with 256 steps by PWM registers" (p.17). A dot lights while its CSy sink is on in its row's slot; PWM 00h is dark.
- Software shutdown (SSD = 0): all current sources off, matrix blanked, registers readable and writable (p.18).
- Hardware shutdown (SDB low): analog circuits off, "the state function registers can be accessed" (p.16, p.18); "a rising edge of SDB resets the I2C bus" and takes the chip out of
  hardware shutdown; no I2C traffic 10 us before and after the rising edge (p.16 Figure 12).
- Power-on reset: registers to defaults when VCC exceeds 2.4 V typ (p.16).

## What the device models, and what it does not

Modeled (`leddriver.json`, device `IS31FL3707`, three parts): the I2C bus and the AD address strap; the 71 PWM registers, the 8 scaling registers, A0h, A1h, B0h, B1h, B2h, B3h, B4h-B5h (read only),
B6h and CFh with their reset values and fields; the pointer with auto-increment on write and read, through the unused addresses (the pointer steps through 03h-CFh by one; the unused ones are
read-only registers that read 0); the reset by writing AEh to CFh; the 8-row scan SW1-SW8 at 29.93 us a row (slot 0 at time 0); software shutdown (SSD = 0: every row low, every sink off).
CS1-CS7 and pin 18 are the columns, active low (a sink pulls its column low).

Known gaps, in the order that matters:

1. **A dot never lights.** The `matrix` driver takes the lit state of a dot from one bit of a field; the chip lights a dot when its PWM byte is nonzero. The dot bits (`DOTS1`-`DOTS8`,
   internal, bit y-1 of DOTSx = dot (SWx, CSy)) are never written by the bus, so every column stays high in every slot. (`engineNeeds` 1.)
2. Pin 18 is CS8 only. In the 7 x 9 modes (SWS 0000, 1000 and the "others") it is SW9; the model always scans 8 rows and treats pin 18 as a column. (`engineNeeds` 2.)
3. The SWS row count (n = 2-8) does not shorten the scan or stop rows from showing data: the round is always 8 rows, 239.44 us. (`engineNeeds` 2.)
4. SDB low blanks the columns but the rows keep scanning; the chip stops its switches. (`shutdown` of a device is an AND of its conditions; this needs an OR, `engineNeeds` 3.) A floating SDB reads
   like a low one (`levels`), which blanks; drive it high.
5. Not modeled at all: the PWM frequency (B2h; the row period stays 29.93 us), PWM duty, scaling and global current, ISET, the SWPDV / CSPUV pull voltages and their de-ghost, spread spectrum,
   the 180 degree phase, open / short detection (OSDE, B3h-B5h read 0), and the I2C reset on the SDB rising edge.
6. The reserved addresses' behavior (read value, effect of a write) is not in the sheet; the model reads 0 and drops writes. Addresses 00h-02h are not registers of the model (a write there is dropped
   and the pointer does not move on).

# TLC6983

## What it is

48 x 16 common-cathode matrix LED display driver: 48 constant-current sources (R0-R15, G0-G15, B0-B15: 16 RGB pixels per line) and 16 line (scan) switches LINE0-LINE15 with 190 mOhm Rds(on)
(`tlc6983` p.1, p.5-6). One device drives 16 x 16 RGB pixels; two stacked drive 32 x 32, three 32 x 48 (p.13-15). 1 to 32 scan lines (SCAN_NUM), built-in SRAM in two banks, up to 16-bit PWM
grayscale, 3-bit global current BC, 8-bit per-colour current CC_R/G/B, 0.2 mA to 20 mA per channel, separate supplies VR, VG, VB for the LED colours (p.1). Interface: the CCSI, three wires
SCLK / SIN / SOUT, 25 MHz max external clock with dual-edge sampling (p.1, p.24).

Supply: VCC 2.5 V to 5.5 V; VR/VG/VB 2.5 V to 5.5 V (p.5 Recommended operating conditions); VIH 0.7 x VCC, VIL 0.3 x VCC on SCLK, SIN; SOUT drives +-2 mA (p.5); -40 to +85 C. Under-voltage shutdown 2.0 V
falling, restart 2.3 V (p.6). IREF resistor sets the current; shorting IREF to GND forces every output off (p.4).

## Current setting (p.15-16)

## CCSI (p.24-28)

Continuous clock series interface: SCLK must run all the time (it is also the clock of the internal frequency multiplier that makes GCLK, p.24, p.45); data are sampled on both edges of SCLK (dual edge,
tsu 10 ns and th 2 ns to each edge, p.9, Figure 7-1); SIN idles high. No chip select.

**Frame (Figure 8-14, p.24-25).** IDLE (SIN high) - START (SIN falls) - DATA - END (18 consecutive high bits on SIN) - IDLE (an END may be followed at once by the next START). DATA is
Head_bytes (one 16-bit command ID plus one check bit) then Data_bytes: N x (3 x 17 bits) for N cascaded devices (p.24). **Every 17-bit word is 16 data bits MSB first followed by a check bit
equal to the NOT of the 16th bit** ("avoid continuous 18-bit HIGH", p.24; Figure 8-18 shows HB15 ... HB0 CHECK).

**Chain (p.24-28).** Up to 32 devices in a chain (SOUT of one to SIN of the next). After power-up the controller sends the **chip index** command; each device takes the next index
(00h up) and passes a changed command on (Figures 8-15, p.25). Commands are then **broadcast** (all devices get the same data: used for the FC registers, read-LOD/LSD commands, Figure 8-17/8-18, p.27)
or **non-broadcast** (each device gets its own data, in the reverse of the cascade order, each device removes the last 51 bits, 3 x 17, before the END: used for the SRAM write, Figure 8-19/8-20, p.27-28).
A read command makes each device insert its 48 bits (3 x 17 bits) before the END, MSB first (Figure 8-21, p.28).

**Data word order.** A 48-bit unit is sent as three 16-bit words: bits 47-32 ("B" in Figure 8-18), bits 31-16 ("G"), bits 15-0 ("R"); each MSB first ("Blue data is the first to be transmitted, then Green and Red", p.27).
Table 9-4 (p.50) shows the FC registers sent as three 16-bit hex groups, e.g. FC0 1000 583F 0107h.

## Display memory and refresh (p.29-31, p.50-51)

- Two banks A and B of grayscale memory. In each frame one is displayed while the other receives the next frame; **each VSYNC command toggles BANK_SEL** (reset 0: bank A is written, bank B displayed) (p.29, Figure 8-22).
- A bank holds 32 lines x 16 memory units; **each unit is 48 bits = the 16-bit grayscale of one pixel's R, G and B** (p.30). One WRTGS data write ("the first WRTGS command") latches the 48 bits into the unit
  R0/G0/B0 of line 0; the write counters `CHANNEL_COUNT` and `LINE_COUNT` (reset 0 at power-up and at VSYNC) then step: after the 16th WRTGS (channel 15) CHANNEL_COUNT returns to 0 and LINE_COUNT goes up by 1, until the
  number of lines set by SCAN_NUM is written (p.30-31). "The total number of memory units that must be written in one bank is 48 x lines" (p.29; this counts channel values, 3 per WRTGS).
- A line of 16 pixels is therefore 16 non-broadcast WRTGS frames, pixel 0 first; each frame carries one unit per chained device (Figure 9-4, p.51).
- Refresh (DS-PWM, p.31): a frame is SUBP_NUM sub-periods (16-128); each sub-period is one segment per scan line; a segment is SEG_LENGTH GCLKs of grayscale display plus T_SW GCLKs (LINE_SWT) of line switching. A pixel's gray value (16 bits)
  sets how many sub-periods and GCLKs it is on. Example (p.46-49): 14-bit PWM, 120 Hz frame, 3840 Hz refresh, 32 sub-periods, 30 lines, GCLK 83.4 MHz.
- Power saving: when a whole next frame is zero the device enters power-saving at VSYNC and all channels are off; any nonzero data wakes it at once (p.24, Figure 8-13).
- Display polarity: the selected LINE switch connects its line to ground; the R/G/B constant-current outputs source current from VLED into the pixel (common cathode, Figure 8-1 p.13, Figure 8-8 p.20). A pixel (line L, channel group n) is lit while
  line L is selected and its colour output n is on.

Host sequence (Figure 9-3, p.50): power the supplies, keep SCLK running, write Chip Index, write FC0/FC1/FC3 (broadcast) - for example Table 9-4 (p.50): FC0 1000 583F 0107h, FC1 2AE0 0094 A631h, FC3 003B 54A9 FF00h - then each frame: write SRAM data for the next frame
(non-broadcast WRTGS), wait for the end of the current frame, write VSYNC; the registers can be read at any time.

## Diagnostics (p.18-22)

LED open detection (LOD): compares OUTn to LODVTH while the output is on; result per line in FC12 (LOD_LINE_WARN) and per channel in FC14 after LOD_LINE_CMD in FC10 selects the line (steps in Figure 8-7, p.19, wait one sub-period between steps 2 and 3);
LODRM_EN removes the open-LED caterpillar for up to three lines (p.20). LED short / weak-short detection (LSD): FC13, FC15, FC11, LSDVTH, LSD_RM (p.20-22). Thermal shutdown 170 C (hysteresis to 155 C), IREF-short protection
(IREF below 0.19 V typ turns the outputs off, above 0.325 V resumes) (p.17, p.23-24).

## Why there is no TLC6983 device

1. **The CCSI is not a bus style of the converter engine.** There is no chip select; a transaction is an in-band START (SIN falls after idle) and END (18 high bits); every word has a 17th check bit; both SCLK edges carry data; the clock never stops; devices chain
   and take an index. `bus.style` knows `spi` (needs `cs`), `i2c`, `i2c-spi`, `parallel`, `clocked`; nothing fits. (`engineNeeds`.)
2. **The display RAM needs write counters and two banks.** A WRTGS command writes the unit at (LINE_COUNT, CHANNEL_COUNT) and advances the counters; VSYNC swaps the banks and clears the counters. The engine writes registers by address or pointer, not by a counter that
   survives across commands.
3. **The `matrix` driver is too small.** At most 16 columns (the chip has 48 outputs), the lit state is one bit of a field (the chip's is a 16-bit gray, nonzero or by its duty), the scan slot is a fixed `periodUs` (the chip's is
   (SEG_LENGTH + T_SW) / (FREQ_MUL x f_SCLK) and a frame has SUBP_NUM sub-periods; the number of lines is a register).
4. What a model of the host-visible part would be: SCLK/SIN/SOUT framed as above; chip index; FC0-FC4 and FC10-FC15 as 48-bit registers with the reset values above; WRTGS into a banked 32 x 16 x 48-bit RAM with the counters; VSYNC; soft reset; read-back of the
   registers; line scan LINE0-LINE(SCAN_NUM) low-side one at a time and the colour outputs of the line's pixels on while the pixel's gray is nonzero. Not modeled even then: gamma / the DS-PWM algorithm and the 16-bit gray levels (only zero versus nonzero),
   the low-gray enhancement, decoupling, de-ghosting and slew-rate registers (stored only), LED open / short / weak-short detection (FC12-FC15 read 0), BC / CC / IMAX current (stored only), SRAM auto-compensation, power saving, thermal and IREF protection.
